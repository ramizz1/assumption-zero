I want to build **TrustLayer**, a platform where small businesses can connect their website, cloud accounts, and GitHub repositories to receive an automatic AI-powered cybersecurity audit.

The platform will:

* Scan websites, APIs, cloud configurations, dependencies, and source code.
* Identify vulnerabilities, exposed secrets, configuration mistakes, outdated packages, and compliance risks.
* Explain every issue in simple language.
* Generate step-by-step remediation instructions.
* Allow verified cybersecurity professionals to offer paid help fixing discovered problems.
* Rescan systems and produce security-score reports.
* Provide downloadable reports for clients, investors, and insurance companies.
* Offer a free open-source CLI and a paid web platform.

Target customers are startups and small businesses with 2–100 employees that cannot afford a full-time security team. The initial markets are Azerbaijan, Turkey, Georgia, and later Europe.

The founders are two university students with cybersecurity and software-development experience. The initial budget is $3,000. We want to build the MVP in three months and reach $10,000 monthly recurring revenue within the first year.

Proposed pricing:

* Free: one website scan per month.
* Starter: $19 per month.
* Business: $79 per month.
* Agency: $249 per month.
* Cybersecurity professionals pay a 15% commission on work obtained through the platform.

Analyse this startup with maximum honesty. Do not assume that AI-generated security findings are accurate. Identify every important assumption concerning demand, willingness to pay, scanning permission, false positives, false negatives, legal liability, data privacy, access to source code, cloud credentials, trust, compliance, competition, customer acquisition, and technical feasibility.

Research current competitors and distinguish between direct competitors, open-source alternatives, cloud-native tools, code scanners, vulnerability scanners, compliance platforms, and cybersecurity consultancies.

Determine:

1. Whether the problem is painful enough for customers to pay.
2. Whether the product attempts to combine too many businesses.
3. Which customer segment should be targeted first.
4. What the smallest safe and useful MVP should contain.
5. Which scanning capabilities should not be included initially.
6. Whether the professional marketplace should be launched immediately or postponed.
7. What could create a defensible advantage when established security scanners already exist.
8. Realistic development and operating costs.
9. A realistic path to the first 10, 100, and 1,000 paying customers.
10. Whether $10,000 MRR in one year is plausible.
11. The three assumptions most likely to kill the business.
12. Experiments that can test those assumptions before building the full product.

Create pessimistic, base, and optimistic financial scenarios. Separate verified facts from assumptions. Cite current sources, show calculations, and never invent market statistics. Conclude with one of these verdicts: **build now, validate before building, significantly narrow the idea, pivot, or abandon**.
